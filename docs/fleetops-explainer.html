AR Technology is the transport contractor for PDO (Petroleum Development Oman) oilfield sites. Their job: move workers, engineers, and equipment safely between Marmul, Nimr, Fahud, and dozens of smaller sites — every single day. If a vehicle breaks down and nobody knows where it is, people can die from heat exposure within hours. FleetOps is the operating system for preventing that.

⏱️ Every 30 seconds, each vehicle sends GPS coordinates, speed, heading, engine status, and odometer. If a vehicle goes silent for over 60 seconds, the map immediately shows "device offline".
IVMS = In-Vehicle Monitoring System

A hardware black box, bolted into the dashboard. Has its own SIM card (doesn't rely on driver's phone), GPS chip, accelerometer, and input for panic button. Sends data even if the driver's phone is dead or out of coverage.

Why not the driver's phone?

Phone GPS is unreliable — phones die, drivers forget to open the app, and cell coverage disappears mid-desert. The IVMS black box has its own SIM card, its own GPS chip, and its own power supply. It works regardless of what the driver does with their phone.

Designed for remote desert conditions

The communication protocol used between the IVMS device and the server is designed specifically for unstable, low-signal networks — 2G, satellite, or sparse coverage. It's lightweight and resilient, built for exactly the conditions between Marmul and Fahud.

Geofences

Every site has a virtual boundary (polygon) stored in PostGIS. When a vehicle crosses the boundary, an event fires — journey arrival confirmed, or "vehicle left approved zone" alert. No manual monitoring required.

Section 06 — The Safety Core

The 6 gates.
All must pass.

A journey cannot be submitted unless all 6 gates pass. The server checks them. No override button exists anywhere in the UI.

This is not a form. These are server-enforced checks. If any gate fails, the server blocks the journey — full stop. Even if someone bypassed the interface entirely, the server would still reject it. There is no way to approve an unsafe journey.
CONDITIONAL means a vehicle has a minor issue but is still driveable — e.g. worn wipers, cracked mirror. Maintenance sets an expiry time. At expiry, vehicle auto-reverts to NO-GO with no manual action needed.

The HSE console receives the WebSocket push. A full-screen alert shows: Mohammed's exact GPS coordinates, vehicle plate, passengers on board (Sara, 1 passenger), current speed (0 — vehicle is stopped), and time since last normal ping.

📱

Three alerts fire simultaneously. SMS to Fatima's mobile. WhatsApp to the emergency response group. Push notification to Ahmad's phone. All delivered within 3–5 seconds of the panic event.

T + 5s
📋
Playbook

Response playbook activates

A structured state machine opens: Step 1 — Confirm contact attempt with vehicle. Step 2 — Deploy rescue vehicle. Step 3 — Notify PDO emergency line. Step 4 — Log resolution and root cause. Each step is time-stamped and assigned to a person.

T + 10m
📡
Escalation

Automatic escalation if no response

If no playbook step is completed within 10 minutes, the system escalates — next-level manager is notified, PDO emergency contact is triggered. Every minute of inaction after that point is permanently recorded in the audit log.

No panic event is ever delayed. Every other operation in the system — notifications, reports, scheduled jobs — is queued for performance. Panic skips all of it. It is handled immediately because seconds matter in the desert.
Section 08 — Design Decisions

Why a web app?
Why not just use X?

Every "why not WhatsApp / spreadsheet / paper / native app" question, answered directly.

Why not just a spreadsheet?
Spreadsheets cannot enforce rules. Anyone can type "approved" in a cell. The server is the only entity that can reject a journey — spreadsheets just describe data. And they have no live tracking, no real-time notifications, and no audit trail.
Why not WhatsApp / phone calls?
In a post-incident investigation, "Khalid sent a voice note saying it was fine" is not legal evidence. The app creates a tamper-evident, timestamped record of every decision and every check. That record is what protects the company when regulators come knocking.
Why web and not a native desktop app?
Web runs on anything with a browser — the laptop in the operations center, the tablet at the depot, the iPad in the HSE office. No installation, no update rollout, works on Windows, Mac, iOS, and Android. One codebase, all devices.
Is there a mobile app too?
Yes. A mobile app for drivers and passengers — people in the field on phones. The web app is for office roles (Journey Manager, HSE, Admin, Maintenance) on desktops. Both connect to the same system — different screen, same rules.
Why not buy Samsara / Verizon Connect?
Commercial fleet systems handle GPS. They don't enforce Oman-specific document types (Mulkia, RAS), don't have the 6-gate journey model, don't integrate with PDO's site permit workflow, and cost $150–200/vehicle/month. The custom build costs less and fits the exact process.
Is FleetOps just "monitoring"?
Monitoring is the least important thing it does. The real value: enforcement (server blocks unsafe trips), accountability (every decision logged with actor + timestamp), and response (panic → playbook → resolution, all documented).
Where does it run?
On a single server — either a VPS in Muscat, or a physical server at the operations center. Everything runs on that one machine. It works offline if the internet goes down. No subscription to external cloud services. AR Technology owns the data and the infrastructure.
